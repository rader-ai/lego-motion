# Story and sound: brick notes

The clay studio's `STORY.md` and `SOUND.md` apply in full. This file adds what bricks change. Sources: research/02 sections 1, 4 and 8; research/08 section 5.

## Story

- **Story counts as much as animation, as far as we can tell.** Brick film festivals give separate awards for story, sound, set design, cinematography and animation, so animation is one axis of seven or eight; no jury has published how it weighs them (research/02 section 1, inferred). Admired short brick films are praised for writing and economy more than brick count.
- **The brick world's own theme** is building freely against following the instructions, and a toy world that knows it's made of toys (research/08 section 5). Good material, not a rule.
- **Gags live in parts:** a piece used for the wrong job, a figure that can't reach the wheel, a build that's one brick short. Keep them visual.
- **Boards from Blender** use the production's validated models in greybox as early as possible, so every board panel is buildable from the start (clay STORY.md section 3, with LDraw models in place of greybox clay).
- **Write for rigid acting.** Scenes that need hugs, clapping, fine hand work or elbows need a staging plan in the script notes (turn away, cut, swap parts; MOTION.md section 2).
- **Dialogue first.** Record or generate scratch voices, lock timing, then animate to it (research/02 section 4).

## Sound

- **Plastic foley is the brick world's signature.** Every stud contact (foot plant, hand grip, part snapping on) gets a short click; plate on plate a hollow clack; heavier parts a lower, louder click; debris a small rattle. Vary pitch per hit so nothing repeats. These conventions are our proposal [U]; no source documented how the features recorded them.
- **Record our own.** Ten minutes at the end of Chris's brick shoot (GOLD_SET.md): snap, click, drop and rattle real bricks on a hard table with the phone close. We own them, they cost nothing, and they match our bricks.
- **Events from the engine.** The stud solver, attach and detach, and builds-itself write click events with part size and material; the Sound Designer places foley from those, so sync is exact.
- **Brick FX sound** is the effect's sound with a plastic layer underneath: an explosion is a whoomp plus a scatter of clacks.
- **Festivals:** at least one major brick film festival bans AI-generated assets while allowing Blender renders (research/02 section 1). If Chris ever wants to enter one, check its rules: voices, music and any generated element may need to be human-made.
